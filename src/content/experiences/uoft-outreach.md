## Context

UofT Engineering Outreach runs DEEP Summer Engineering School: high-school students living on campus for a few weeks of engineering classes, labs, and city life. I was a residence counsellor for the summer: safety, nights on call, and the quieter work of making a floor of strangers feel like a place people actually wanted to come back to.

Two hundred-plus students on campus. Office hours don’t cover everything that happens after dinner.

## What the job was

### Safety and security

Day to day, a lot of the role was looking after safety and security for the residence. That meant knowing who was on the floor, noticing when something felt off, and staying visible enough that students knew who to find if they needed help.

### On call

I held the on-call phone for crisis response and the ordinary residence issues that don’t wait for a morning meeting: lockouts, conflict, someone who’s having a rough night. The work was less about drama and more about being reachable, calm, and clear when the building was quiet and something still needed a decision.

### Conduct and trust

The cohort was mixed: different ages, hometowns, and comfort levels with living away from home. Part of the job was building enough trust that people would talk to you early, and still holding the line on conduct when it mattered, without turning the floor into a place that felt cold or policed.

## Community and outings

The soft side of the job mattered as much as the phone. Floor energy, someone to talk to, and outings that got people off campus and into the city together: baseball at Rogers Centre, nights under the CN Tower, the small group moments that stick after the program ends.
